Project management case studies

Case 1: Scope realignment & margin protection

Project Manager · Fixed-price · 55% margin preserved · 3-month extension

The challenge

Mid-delivery, executive stakeholders escalated two scope gaps: expecting advanced ML instead of the agreed deterministic automation, and missing several uncontracted edge cases. Attempting unstandardized ML work threatened project collapse on a fixed-fee budget.

The action

  • Reconciled sales, discovery, and contract baselines internally before addressing the client
  • Absorbed minor internal delivery misses at no cost to build goodwill
  • Proved ML was technically unfeasible without prior data standardization, defining a clear architectural boundary
  • Structured the standardization and ML work into a separate, paid project phase

The outcome

  • Protected profitability: maintained a 55% delivery margin by preventing severe scope creep
  • Account growth: signed a 3-month paid follow-on SOW for Phase 2 implementation

Process retrospective

Instituted a pre-sales delivery gate to strictly separate "automation" vs. "AI/ML" scoping language upfront.

Case 2: Rapid team scaling & platform migration

Dedicated PM · SAFe/Agile · On-time delivery · 10 engineers onboarded · Stage 2 SOW won

The challenge

Mid-program, an enterprise platform migration for a global Oil & Gas client faced a budget-to-scope deficit. The remaining allocated funds were insufficient to hit the hard deadline with the existing 15-person team, placing the release schedule and executive relationship at risk.

The action

  • Audited and re-baselined the backlog with the Lead BA and Solution Architect to isolate critical path deliverables
  • Modeled three scenario forecasts for leadership: reduce scope, extend timeline, or scale team capacity
  • Led the decision to scale, directly flagging Brooks's Law ramp risks and structuring a 3-week onboarding buffer
  • Recruited, integrated, and restructured 10 new engineers/QAs within 4 weeks (15 → 25), organizing delivery into two parallel, autonomous pods

The outcome

  • Hit critical deadline: delivered 100% of high- and medium-priority scope on time with only a modest, planned budget variance
  • Account expansion: high delivery confidence directly secured a follow-on SOW for Stage 2

Process retrospective

The 3-week onboarding buffer strained lead engineers who were ramping newcomers while delivering; identified that future scale-ups require a 4-to-6 week staggered onboarding window.

Case 3: Client de-escalation & account recovery

Engagement Manager · Enterprise SaaS · Escalation defused · Re-review delivered · Account trust restored

The challenge

Following a post-implementation system review, an enterprise customer rejected the initial findings, feeling the advice was abstract, misaligned with their operational reality, and unhelpful. The customer disengaged, halting the delivery stream and putting account sentiment at risk.

The action

  • Stepped in directly with the client PM and Customer Success to listen to grievances and diagnose why the original recommendations missed the mark
  • Partnered with consulting management to rotate the technical consultant for an SME with stronger advisory and domain communication skills
  • Re-initiated a structured discovery pass focused on translating technical system configurations directly into business benefits the client understood
  • Kept Sales, Consulting Leadership, and CS synchronized throughout the reset to ensure a unified, supportive front

The outcome

  • Delivered & accepted: client fully accepted the revised findings, resetting the engagement tone from defensive to collaborative
  • Protected account: successfully resolved an active escalation without commercial fallout or churn

Process retrospective

Mid-project feedback check-ins had gone unanswered earlier by the customer; reinforced that client silence is an active risk indicator requiring a live touchpoint rather than waiting for formal deliverable handoffs.

Risk management

Sanitized governance excerpt demonstrating quantitative risk prioritization (5×5 severity matrix) and rapid blocker triage during technical delivery

Every risk is scored as Impact × Likelihood (1–5 each) to produce a Severity index from 1–25, then routed to one of four strategies — Accept, Mitigate, Transfer, or Avoid — so prioritization is a calculation, not a judgment call. In parallel, live blockers and dependencies are logged and triaged separately from long-range risk, so day-to-day delivery friction gets resolved fast without polluting the strategic risk view.

Risk management approach

Risk vs. issue

A risk is a future event that could negatively affect schedule or cost. An issue is a problem that already exists today and needs immediate handling.

Scoring

Likelihood (0–5) × Impact (0–5) = Severity (0–25) — a consistent, comparable score for ranking every risk on the register.

Status flow

Active (identified, under observation) → In progress (triggered, mitigation underway) → Closed (no longer relevant).

Mitigation strategy

Every risk is assigned one of four responses: Accept, Mitigate, Transfer, or Avoid — decided at the point of entry, not after the fact.

Ownership

Each risk has a named owner responsible for monitoring it and executing the mitigation plan.

Early warning

Symptoms (leading indicators) are logged against each risk, so a trigger event prompts action before the risk becomes an issue.
